# COST.md — build spec for the single live cost / funding truth (cross-sectional-momentum book)

This file is the authoritative build spec for the COST pillar under the
**cross-sectional-momentum pivot**. Build to the contracts and invariants
below. Where this file and your own instinct disagree, this file wins. Ask
before deviating from any **Invariant**.

> **Rewrite notice.** The prior version of this file priced a single
> directional trade's round-trip (entry leg, hold, exit leg) against that
> trade's own stop/target R-multiple. That system (breakout/trend timing
> entries on 1-minute bars) is **PROVEN DEAD**: gross directional R ≈ 0 across
> every timeframe, exit rule, filter, leverage setting, and 3 asset classes
> tested, even under inversion (`vol_research` synthesis; VolArb backtest
> finding). The risk unit it priced no longer exists. This rewrite prices the
> risk unit of the system that **is** live: a weekly-rebalanced, dollar-neutral,
> cross-sectional momentum book on Binance USDT-M perpetuals. The math that
> survives the pivot — the additive fee/half-spread/impact/funding
> decomposition, the square-root impact law, the funding sign convention, the
> EWMA/CI machinery, DSR/CPCV validation — is salvaged verbatim where the
> *mechanism* is identical and only the object being costed has changed from
> "one trade's R" to "one week's portfolio return."

This module is the **single cost truth** consumed by every other pillar in the
xsec-momentum system:
- The **signal** module (`src/convexity/signal/signals.py`) decides direction and
  cross-sectional rank; it does not know about costs.
- The **portfolio** module (`src/convexity/portfolio/portfolio.py`) decides target
  weights (quantile membership, within-leg weighting); it does not know about
  costs.
- **COST (this) is a PROVIDER, not a decider.** It answers exactly one family
  of questions and emits numbers the accounting/promotion layers consume:

> Given fees, the square-root spread/impact law, and Binance's signed 8h
> funding stamps — what does REBALANCING this week's target book cost, what
> funding does the book earn or pay while held, and does the net-of-cost weekly
> return series still clear a real Sharpe bar once turnover and impact are
> priced honestly?

COST never sets a weight, never vetoes a rebalance, and never chooses a
lookback. It hands the **accounting** layer
(`src/convexity/cost/accounting.py::settle_week`) a **per-unit-turnover friction
rate** and a **signed funding cash flow**, and it hands the **SYSTEM promotion
gate** (`src/convexity/system/validate/`) a **friction-stress scenario** (§8)
so that "the edge survives cost" is tested at 1.5× the baseline friction
estimate, not merely at the point estimate.

Scope: crypto only. The live edge is the Binance USDT-M perpetual
cross-sectional momentum sleeve (`Convexity xsec momentum edge`,
`convexity-xsec-momentum-edge.md`). The equity/commodity sleeves in
`src/convexity/panel/sleeve.py` are momentum-only research comparators with their
own hand-set `ClassFriction` constants and are **out of scope** for this
spec's cost *model* (they use flat conservative bps, not the derived
square-root/Amihud machinery below) — this spec governs the crypto sleeve's
cost truth, which is also the one production book.

Context: crypto cross-sectional momentum book, weekly (Friday-close)
rebalance, ~50 liquid USDT-M perps, dollar-neutral quintile long/short,
inverse-realized-vol weighted. This is **not** a 1-minute intraday system —
the friction budget is paid **~52 times per year**, not thousands of times, so
the cost model's job is to price *turnover* honestly, not to minimize latency.
Every live number must be `F_t`-measurable (causal); future data appears only
inside firewalled validation code (`src/convexity/system/xsec_validate.py`,
`src/convexity/system/cpcv.py`).

**The filtration `F_t` (defined once, used precisely everywhere below).**
Fix the weekly rebalance grid `t = 0, 1, 2, …` (Friday UTC close,
`src/convexity/portfolio/engine.py::_first_friday_index`), identical to `PORTFOLIO.md
§2.1` and `LEVERAGE.md §2.1`. `F_t` is the filtration (information set)
generated by: (i) all daily OHLCV closes and dollar volumes up to and
including day `t`'s close (`src/convexity/panel/panel.py`), (ii) all **completed**
8h funding stamps up to and including `t`'s close — for a Friday-close
rebalance this includes that week's own just-realized stamps used to settle
the week just ending, but never a stamp timestamped after `t`, and (iii) the
point-in-time `listed`/eligibility mask up to and including `t` (a coin not
yet listed, or already delisted, at `t` is not in `F_t`). Concretely,
`ADV_i` and `Amihud_i` (§5.2–5.3) are computed over a trailing window ending
at day `t` inclusive — the window's right edge is `t`, never `t+1` or later
— and `w_i,t-1_carried` (the pre-rebalance weight used to compute turnover,
§5.6) is itself `F_t`-measurable because it was fixed at `t-1` and only
*drifted* by realized (hence already-in-`F_t`) price/funding moves through
`t`. Every quantity this spec calls a "rate" or "input" (`fee`,
`half_spread_i`, `Amihud_i`, `ADV_i`, `round_trip_friction_i`) is a function
of `F_t` only. What is **not** in `F_t`, and must never leak into a cost
input: `r_{i,t+1}` (the week-ahead price return) and `f_i(τ_k)` for any
stamp `τ_k > t` — both are realized only as of `t+1` and are consumed
exclusively inside the firewalled validation code (`src/convexity/system/xsec_validate.py`,
`src/convexity/system/cpcv.py`), never inside the cost estimators of §5–§6.

## 0. Governing principles (read before anything else)

**P1 — One cost truth, one risk unit: the WEEKLY portfolio return, not the
trade R-multiple.** The old risk unit ("does this trade's expected favorable
excursion clear its round-trip cost before the stop?") does not exist in a
book with no stops, no single-trade P&L, and no trade-level entry/exit
signal. The unit priced here is **next week's net portfolio return**:

```
net_t = gross_price_t + funding_t - cost_t
```

exactly the decomposition already implemented in
`src/convexity/cost/accounting.py::WeekResult` / `settle_week`. **Notation
bridge to `SYSTEM.md`/`PORTFOLIO.md`/`LEVERAGE.md`**: this spec deliberately
names each term after its `accounting.py` code variable (`net_t`,
`gross_price_t`, `funding_t`, `cost_t`) rather than `SYSTEM.md §1.1`'s
math-register `R_k := r_{k+1}^{price} + r_{k+1}^{funding} − c_{k+1}^{friction}`,
because COST's job is to be checkable directly against the running code
(§1's Invariant N). The two notations name the identical quantities at the
identical rebalance clock: `net_t ≡ R_k`, `gross_price_t ≡ r_{k+1}^{price}`,
`funding_t ≡ r_{k+1}^{funding}`, `cost_t ≡ c_{k+1}^{friction}`, with `t`
here and `τ_k`/week-index `k` there both denoting the same Friday-close
rebalance grid (`SYSTEM.md §1.1`, `PORTFOLIO.md §2.1`) — a reader moving
between specs should treat the subscript-timing convention (COST's `t` for
"the week settled at `t`" vs. SYSTEM's `k`/`k+1` for "decided at `τ_k`,
realized at `τ_{k+1}`") as a labeling difference only, never a different
economic quantity. COST supplies `cost_t` (this spec, §5) and the signed
`funding_t` (§6); `gross_price_t` is the signal/portfolio layer's business,
not COST's. There is exactly one friction rate and one funding model
consumed by every one of the 52
rebalances a year — not a per-trade stub re-derived at each name's entry.

**P2 — Provider, never decider.** COST emits a friction rate (bps or
fraction of notional) and a signed funding cash flow. It never emits a
weight, a veto, or a "skip this rebalance" flag. The portfolio module forms
`w_target` from the rank vector with zero knowledge of cost; COST prices
*whatever turnover that choice produces* after the fact. If a config is too
expensive, that shows up as a lower net Sharpe in validation (§8), not as a
COST-side rejection of the rebalance.

**P3 — Cost is an ADDITIVE decomposition of independent, auditable dollar
terms, applied to ACTUAL turnover.** Round-trip friction per rebalance is:

```
cost_t = Σ_i |w_i,t − w_i,t-1_carried| · (2 · (fee + half_spread_i/2 + impact_i))
```

summed over **every name whose weight changed** — names entering a leg,
names leaving a leg, AND names staying in a leg but drifting weight — not a
fixed per-name assumption and not a flat "cost per name traded." This is
`src/convexity/cost/accounting.py::settle_week`'s `turnover = 0.5·Σ|w_target −
w_carried|` and `cost = turnover · friction` (scalar-friction path) or
`cost = 0.5·Σ|dw_i|·friction_i` (per-symbol path, §5.6). There is NO learned
weighting and no `fee × spread × regime` product — each term has its own
estimator, its own units, and is independently auditable (P3 carries over
unchanged from the prior COST.md; only the object it is applied to changed
from "one trade's notional" to "one rebalance's turnover vector").

**P4 — Funding is charged on REAL wall-clock 8h stamps, signed per leg, and is
empirically a MINOR term.** Funding accrues on Binance's actual 00:00 / 08:00
/ 16:00 UTC funding timestamps (not once per bar, not once per week as a
single lump), summed and signed per name over the holding week. Confirmed
empirically (`Convexity xsec momentum edge` digest): funding is **~3–5% of
gross** return relative to friction, which is **~10× larger**. Cost
discipline in this system is therefore dominated by **turnover control**, not
funding timing — a conclusion this spec's numbers must reflect, not merely
assert (§6.4).

**P5 — Conservative under uncertainty, but grounded in measurement, not
invention.** Where a cost input is thin or uncertain, COST returns the more
conservative (higher) estimate — never zero, never an optimistic best case.
But "conservative" here means *anchored to the measured Amihud/spread
constants of §5*, not an arbitrarily large made-up number. Every constant in
this document is either (a) derived from a cited formula or (b) taken
verbatim from the empirical baseline (cited by name, e.g. "taker = 5bp,
Binance USDT-M VIP0").

**P6 — Sign discipline is load-bearing (unchanged from the prior COST.md).**
Funding is SIGNED relative to the position. A perp long paying positive
funding bleeds carry; a perp short in the same regime EARNS it — this is the
mechanism the funding-carry satellite signal (and the momentum book's
incidental funding P&L) both depend on, and a sign error silently turns a
cost line into a rebate. Sign is unit-tested at the boundary (Invariant P).

**P7 — NO ML, closed-form only.** Every estimator in this spec — EWMA,
square-root impact, Amihud ratio, block-bootstrap CI — is closed-form
classical statistics. No fitted/learned cost model, no gradient-boosted
impact predictor, no per-name hyperparameter search on realized P&L. This
mirrors `pyproject.toml`'s "scipy: closed-form (NOT ML)" doctrine and the
project-wide NO-ML constraint.

Forbidden in v1: any ML/learned cost or impact model (P7); pricing cost as a
fixed per-name assumption instead of actual turnover (P3); confusing accrued
(FACT) and forecast funding, or booking funding at the wrong sign (P6);
Corwin-Schultz high-low spread estimation on 1-minute-aggregated daily OHLC as
an impact proxy — **tried and rejected**, see §5.4.3, a documented dead end;
applying `/1e4` twice or omitting it once across the bps/fraction boundary
(Invariant U-dim, carried from the prior spec); charging funding once per
week as a lump instead of per real 8h stamp (Invariant F-stamp); reporting
the light-cost / single-lookback headline Sharpe as the deployable number
instead of the honest Amihud-cost, lookback-band net Sharpe (§8.4, Invariant
HONEST-SR).

## 2. Architecture: two consumers, one provider

```
                     ┌───────────────────────────────┐
  Panel (price/vol/  │             COST                │
  funding, causal) ─►│  fee tier · sqrt-spread ·        │
                     │  Amihud impact · signed funding  │
                     └──────┬─────────────────┬────────┘
                            │                 │
             per-unit-turnover friction    signed weekly funding
             (accounting.settle_week:      (accounting.settle_week:
              cost_t = turnover · rate)     funding_t = Σ −w_i·f_i)
                            │                 │
                            └────────┬────────┘
                                     ▼
                          net_t = gross_price_t + funding_t − cost_t
                                     │
                                     ▼
                     src/convexity/system/xsec_validate.py (Sharpe, DSR, CPCV)
                                     │
                                     ▼
                     SYSTEM promotion gate + §8 FRICTION STRESS scenario
```

Three layers, kept independent for testability:

1. **Component layer** — three independent estimators, each emitting one cost
   line in fraction-of-notional units: `FeeModel` (§5.1), `SpreadImpactModel`
   (§5.2–5.4, the square-root law + Amihud), `FundingModel` (§6). Each
   testable in isolation against the empirical baseline.
2. **Assembly layer** — `round_trip_friction(...)` (already implemented,
   `src/convexity/cost/accounting.py`) sums the components into one per-unit-
   turnover rate; `settle_week` applies it to the realized turnover vector.
   COST's job in this layer is to supply the three rate inputs correctly, not
   to re-derive the turnover/settlement arithmetic.
3. **Validation layer** — the friction-stress scenario (§8), which
   re-runs `src/convexity/system/xsec_validate.py` at 1.5× baseline friction and feeds a
   pass/fail into SYSTEM's promotion criteria (`src/convexity/system/validate/promotion.py`).

## 4. Parameter governance

- **RED — shared economic constants, not per-instrument.** The taker fee
  (5bp, VIP0, §5.1); the spread-anchor constant `c` in the square-root
  half-spread proxy (§5.2, calibrated so the median liquid name pays ~1bp);
  the Amihud-to-impact-bps conversion constant (§5.3); the friction-stress
  multiplier (1.5×, §8); the promotion-gate Sharpe floor evaluated under
  stress (§8.2).
- **AMBER — supplied market metadata, per instrument.** Each symbol's
  trailing dollar-volume `ADV_i` (from the panel, causal); each symbol's
  trailing Amihud illiquidity ratio `Amihud_i` (from the panel, causal); the
  funding interval (8h, Binance USDT-M); the funding cap.
- **TEAL — none in this spec.** There is no per-instrument fitted decision
  parameter in the cost model beyond the AMBER trailing statistics above —
  everything downstream of `ADV_i`/`Amihud_i` is a closed-form transform
  (§5.2, §5.3), not a fit.
- **BLUE — the lookback band itself is governed by SYSTEM, not COST.** COST
  is agnostic to which lookback `L ∈ [12,16]` generated the rank vector; it
  prices whatever turnover that rank vector produces. The BLUE grid

  (`SYSTEM.md §7.1`), not double-counted here.

## 5. The cost decomposition (full math, additive — P3)

### 5.1 Fees

```
fee = 5 bp = 0.0005            (fraction of notional, Binance USDT-M VIP0 taker)
```

This is the real Binance USDT-M VIP0 taker schedule
(`src/convexity/cost/config/fee_schedule.py::BINANCE_USDM_FEE_SCHEDULE`,
`tier_for_volume(0.0).taker_rate == 0.0005`), and it is the **dominant single
term** in the round-trip friction budget (5bp fee vs. ~1bp half-spread and
~0.5bp impact at the calibrated anchor, §5.2–5.3). It is stated here as
**conservative/retail-tier**: BNB fee-discount and higher VIP tiers (reached
by rolling 30-day volume) only ever **lower** this rate, never raise it, so
VIP0-taker is a legitimate worst-case default for a book whose target size
has not yet earned tier discounts. A book that qualifies for a lower tier
gets a strictly better (lower) `cost_t` — never a correction in the other
direction.

### 5.2 Half-spread — the square-root law, anchored empirically

**Derivation (worked, not asserted).** The square-root law of market impact
(Torre & Ferrari 1997, the BARRA/Grinold & Kahn 2000 ch.16 formulation; also
Gatheral 2010 for the no-dynamic-arbitrage argument for why impact/cost must
scale as a concave, sub-linear function of participation/size rather than
linearly) states that the price impact of trading a size `Q` against average
daily volume `ADV` scales as:

```
impact(Q) ∝ σ · sqrt(Q / ADV)
```

The intuition (Grinold & Kahn ch.16): a linear impact model implies a trader
could earn unbounded arbitrage by splitting one large order into infinitely
many infinitesimal slices at zero marginal cost, which contradicts observed
market depth having a *finite* elasticity — cost must grow slower than
linearly in size for a well-posed market, and the square root is both the
simplest concave functional form consistent with observed impact-decay data
and the form that falls out of a Kyle-type model with time-varying liquidity
(Gatheral 2010's "no-dynamic-arbitrage" condition, which shows that any
impact function with a *permanent* component must be linear but a
*temporary/decaying* impact component can be concave, and empirically is
well-fit by a square root). This is the same functional form already used
for the Amihud illiquidity measure below (§5.3), which is not a coincidence:
Amihud's ratio is a discrete-time empirical proxy for exactly this
price-per-unit-volume elasticity.

**Applying it to a bid-ask half-spread proxy.** The touch-crossing half-spread
a resting-liquidity-taking order pays is modeled as inversely proportional to
the square root of the instrument's liquidity (proxied by average dollar
volume `ADV_i`, causal — a trailing window, never realized future volume):

```
half_spread_i = c / sqrt(ADV_i)
```

`c` is a single RED anchor constant, calibrated (not fitted per name — one
shared constant, §4) so that **the median liquid name in the ~50-name
universe pays ~1bp half-spread** — the empirical target stated in the
`Convexity xsec momentum edge` digest and matched by the existing research
default `HALF_SPREAD_FRAC = 1.0e-4` in `src/convexity/cost/accounting.py`. Given
the median-name `ADV_median` in the live universe:

```
c = 1bp · sqrt(ADV_median)
```

so that `half_spread_i = 1bp · sqrt(ADV_median / ADV_i)` — a name with 4×
the median liquidity pays half the median spread; a name with 1/4 the median
liquidity pays double. This is the closed-form anchoring step: **one
calibration point (the median name, ~1bp) plus the sqrt functional form
determines every other name's spread from its own `ADV_i`, with no
per-name fitting.**

**Half-spread cost per leg** (`Notional_leg` is a **dollar** quantity here
and throughout §5 — the dollar size of the trade in name `i` at this
rebalance, i.e. `|Δw_i| · NAV` for a book of net asset value `NAV`; e.g. for
a $1,000,000 NAV book and a name whose weight changes by `|Δw_i| = 0.04`,
`Notional_leg = $40,000` — this concrete scale is used consistently in the
worked example of §5.6/§9 below, stated here rather than only implicitly
via a fractional `|Δw_i|` so the units of every downstream formula in this
section are unambiguous from first use):

```
C_hs_leg = (half_spread_i / 2) · Notional_leg
```

(the round-trip formula in the empirical baseline states the friction as
`2·(fee + half_spread_i/2 + impact_i)` — the `/2` here converts a full
quoted half-spread into the *expected* cost of crossing it on one side,
consistent with `src/convexity/cost/accounting.py`'s existing `HALF_SPREAD_FRAC`
convention of a small flat constant already expressed at that per-leg
scale.)

### 5.3 Impact — measured Amihud illiquidity estimator

**Why Amihud, not Corwin-Schultz (worked negative example, §5.4.3
cross-referenced here).** Impact must be estimated from a REAL illiquidity
measure fit to trailing return/volume data, not assumed at a single flat
constant across all ~50 names — a single flat impact constant cannot
distinguish a top-5-liquidity major from a tail name, and the universe-size
robustness result (`Convexity xsec momentum edge`: N=100 loses to N~50–75
specifically because of "real illiquid-tail cost, ~+2.9%/yr drag from the
added tail") is *only visible* if the impact model actually varies with
each name's realized illiquidity.

The **Amihud (2002) illiquidity ratio** is the classical closed-form
estimator for exactly this:

```
Amihud_i = (1/D) · Σ_{d=1}^{D}  |r_i,d| / DollarVolume_i,d
```

— the average, over a trailing `D`-day window (causal, no lookahead), of the
absolute daily return per dollar of volume traded. It is a direct empirical
measure of "how much does price move per dollar traded," which is *exactly*
what a market-impact coefficient is supposed to capture, estimated from
data the book already has (daily close, daily dollar volume) with **zero
additional data requirement** beyond the OHLCV panel already in
`src/convexity/panel/panel.py`.

**Window length `D` and the stationarity assumption it requires (a
regularity condition missing from the prior draft).** `D` is set to match
`src/convexity/panel/panel.py::trailing_dollar_volume`'s existing `window=30`
(trading days) default — one shared, causal, 30-day trailing window for
both `ADV_i` (§5.2) and `Amihud_i`, rather than a second independently-tuned
window length (avoiding a second per-instrument fitted parameter, P7).
Using a finite trailing window to estimate a "current" illiquidity level is
only a valid causal estimator of `F_t`'s liquidity state **under an implicit
local-stationarity assumption**: `Amihud_i`'s true underlying value is
assumed approximately constant over the trailing 30-day window (else the
average conflates a stale illiquidity regime with the current one). This is
the same assumption every EWMA/trailing-window estimator in this project
makes (no different, in kind, from `LEVERAGE.md`'s trailing realized-vol
estimator), and it is not free: a name whose liquidity is genuinely
regime-shifting inside the 30-day window (e.g. a sudden large delisting
threat or an exchange incentive program that materially changes its
`$Vol_i,d`) will have a `D`-day-averaged `Amihud_i` that lags the true
current level for up to `D` days. §10's `ADV_i → 0` and undefined-`Amihud_i`
edge cases handle the boundary failure modes; this paragraph states the
assumption underlying the non-boundary, ordinary case. No CUSUM or other
change-point re-estimation trigger is added for `Amihud_i`/`ADV_i` in v1 —
that would be new machinery beyond what `panel.py` already computes, and is
noted as a candidate future refinement, not a v1 requirement (P7's
no-per-name-fit constraint keeps this deliberately simple: one shared
window length, no per-name adaptive re-estimation).

**Stability-of-the-liquidity-distribution assumption (also previously
silent).** The `c`/`k_amihud` calibration anchors (§5.2, §5.3) are each a
single constant fit once to "the median liquid name in the ~50-name
universe." This implicitly assumes the **cross-sectional distribution** of
liquidity across the universe (not any one name's time series) is
reasonably stable release-over-release — if the universe's overall
liquidity profile shifted materially (e.g. a broad deleveraging event
compressing `ADV_i` for most names simultaneously, or the universe
construction itself changing what counts as "liquid enough to be eligible,"
`docs/specs/UNIVERSE.md`), the single anchor calibrated at one point in time
would under- or over-price the *median* name until recalibrated. This spec
does not schedule an automatic recalibration cadence for `c`/`k_amihud` in
v1 (recalibrating a RED shared constant is a governance action, §4, not a
per-rebalance computation) — it is recorded here as an explicit, previously
implicit assumption rather than left unstated.

**Converting the Amihud ratio to a per-leg impact fraction — derived, not
merely asserted by analogy.** This is the step the independent review
correctly flagged as under-derived in the prior draft; it is worked in full
here.

Start from what `Amihud_i` actually measures: the *slope* of a linear
price-impact-per-dollar-traded relationship estimated from historical
(non-block) trading,

```
Amihud_i  ≈  E[ |Δp / p| / $Vol ]  =  λ_i        (units: [return]/[dollar])
```

i.e. `Amihud_i` is an estimate of the **local linear-response coefficient**
`λ_i` such that a small, price-taking dollar volume `$v` traded on an
average day moves price by approximately `λ_i · $v` in return units. This
is Amihud's (2002) own interpretation of the ratio: it is explicitly a
*linear*, small-trade illiquidity slope, estimated from the observed
day-to-day co-movement of `|r_i,d|` and `$Vol_i,d}` — it is **not** itself a
statement about how a single large block trade of size `Notional_leg`
(which can be a material fraction of one day's `$Vol_i,d`) moves price,
because `λ_i` was estimated from the linear (small-size) regime.

The square-root law of §5.2 states that the impact of trading a size `Q`
(here `Q = Notional_leg`) against the *same* underlying liquidity is not
`λ_i · Q` (linear, which would extrapolate the small-trade slope `λ_i` into
the large-trade regime it was never estimated in) but a concave function of
`Q` that must equal `λ_i · Q` in the small-`Q` limit (both models must
agree where the linear estimate is valid) while growing sub-linearly as `Q`
approaches an economically meaningful fraction of `ADV_i`. The unique
power-law family `impact(Q) = λ_i^{a} · Q^{b}` that (i) reduces to the
linear form as `b → 1` in the small-`Q` limit implied by Amihud's own
derivation, and (ii) matches the `b = 1/2` concavity of the square-root law
independently derived in §5.2 from the no-arbitrage argument, is obtained by
setting `b = 1/2` and solving for the exponent `a` on `λ_i` from a
**dimensional consistency requirement**: `impact_frac_i` must be
dimensionless (`[return]`), `Q` is in dollars, and `λ_i` is in
`[return]/[dollar]`. Since `sqrt(Q)` alone carries units `sqrt([dollar])`,
matching `[return]` forces `λ_i^a` to carry units `[return] / sqrt([dollar])
= λ_i^1 · [dollar]^{-1/2}`, which is satisfied uniquely by `a = 1` combined
with an extra explicit `sqrt(1/[dollar])`-type conversion factor — i.e. the
functional form is forced, by dimensional analysis alone (worked fully in
§5.4), to be

```
impact_frac_i = k_amihud · sqrt( Amihud_i · Notional_leg )
C_impact_leg  = impact_frac_i · Notional_leg
```

with `k_amihud` absorbing the residual proportionality constant (the same
role `c` plays in §5.2 for the half-spread law — see §5.4 for why
`k_amihud` must specifically carry units `sqrt(1/[return])` for the whole
expression to come out dimensionless). **What is derived here**: the
exponent `1/2` on `Q` (forced by matching §5.2's independently-derived
concavity result) and the exponent `1` on `Amihud_i` under the square root
(forced by requiring the expression reduce to the linear small-`Q` model in
the appropriate limit while remaining dimensionally consistent). **What is
still calibrated, not derived**: the scalar value of `k_amihud` itself —
exactly as `c` in §5.2 is a calibrated anchor rather than a
first-principles physical constant, because neither the square-root law nor
the Amihud ratio pins down an absolute *level* from theory alone, only a
*functional form*. `k_amihud` is the single RED conversion constant (§4),
calibrated the same way as `c` in §5.2: chosen so the median liquid name's
impact matches the empirical anchor of **~0.5bp at the book's typical
rebalance size** (matching `src/convexity/cost/accounting.py::IMPACT_FRAC =
0.5e-4`). This is not a second independent fit — `Amihud_i` supplies the
**cross-sectional shape** (which names are relatively illiquid), and the
single constant `k_amihud` supplies the **level anchor** (matching the
measured research default), exactly mirroring the `c`/`ADV_median`
anchoring pattern of §5.2.

**Regularity condition — participation rate (a required, previously
missing assumption).** The derivation above is only valid in the regime
Amihud's own linear-slope estimate is representative of, extrapolated by
the concave correction to a *moderate* trade size — it is **not** validated,
by this spec or by the underlying research, for a trade whose
`Notional_leg` is a large fraction of the name's own `ADV_i` (e.g.
participation rate `Notional_leg / ADV_i` materially above the low
single-digit percent typical of this book's ~52-rebalance/year, ~50-name,
dollar-neutral turnover profile). **Invariant PARTICIPATION** below states
this bound explicitly; it did not exist in the prior draft.

> **Invariant PARTICIPATION — the sqrt-Amihud impact law is calibrated and
> validated only up to the book's historical participation-rate range.**
> The `~0.5bp at the book's typical rebalance size` anchor (and the `~1bp`
> half-spread anchor of §5.2) are calibrated at the **realized** trade sizes
> of a ~50-name, weekly-turnover, dollar-neutral book — empirically a small
> single-digit-percent-of-`ADV_i` participation rate per rebalance. This
> spec's cost model must NOT be applied unmodified to a book size materially
> larger than what generated the calibration data (§7's capacity discussion
> is the relevant reference point: uncapped median deployable book ≈$3M,
> rising to ≈$12–23M only under liquidity-share capping at tightness
> `C=2–3`) without re-validating that the sqrt-law still tracks realized
> slippage at the new participation rate — a promotion review that
> increases target book size by an order of magnitude must re-run
> `src/convexity/cost/friction/validate/amihud_calibration.py`'s walk-forward check
> at the new size, not merely re-use the existing `k_amihud`/`c` constants.

### 5.4 Impact — worked derivation of the dimensional check

**Dimensions (the bug magnet, carried over from the prior COST.md
verbatim).** `Amihud_i` has units `[return]/[dollar]`. `Amihud_i ·
Notional_leg` therefore has units of `[return]` — a pure fraction, since
`Notional_leg` is in dollars and cancels the Amihud ratio's dollar
denominator. `sqrt(Amihud_i · Notional_leg)` is then `sqrt([return])`, which
is **not yet** a fraction — this is why `k_amihud` must itself carry units
of `sqrt(1/[return])` for `impact_frac_i` to come out dimensionless. In
practice this is handled by calibrating `k_amihud` empirically against the
observed ~0.5bp anchor (as done in §5.3) rather than deriving its physical
units from first principles — the calibration step *is* the unit fix,
exactly as `c` in §5.2 is calibrated rather than derived from a
first-principles liquidity constant. **The invariant this buys**: `C_impact_leg
= impact_frac_i · Notional_leg` is a quote-currency cost with NO further
`/1e4` — the `/1e4` conversion is needed only when reporting `impact_frac_i ·
1e4` as a bps column, mirroring the half-spread bps/fraction boundary of the
prior COST.md's Invariant U-dim.

> **Invariant U-dim — impact fraction multiplies notional directly, no
> `/1e4`.** `impact_frac_i` (from §5.3) is already a return fraction; `C_impact_leg
> = impact_frac_i · Notional_leg` in quote currency, and `impact_bps_leg = 1e4 ·
> impact_frac_i` for the reporting column only. A `/1e4` applied to
> `C_impact_leg` is the historical bug this invariant exists to catch.

#### 5.4.3 The Corwin-Schultz dead end (documented cautionary note)

**What was tried.** Before settling on the Amihud estimator (§5.3), the
Corwin & Schultz (2012) high-low spread estimator was tried as the impact
proxy. Corwin-Schultz infers the bid-ask spread from the ratio of high-low
range over 2 consecutive days, exploiting the fact that the *observed*
high-low range reflects both true volatility and the bid-ask bounce, and
under specific assumptions the two can be separated in closed form.

**Why it failed here.** The Corwin-Schultz estimator was applied to
**1-minute-aggregated daily OHLC** (daily high/low built by aggregating
1-minute bars, the data granularity actually available). This **conflates
realized intrabar volatility with the bid-ask spread**: Corwin-Schultz's
separation identity relies on the high-low range at a *specific* sampling
frequency where the volatility component and the spread component scale
differently with the sampling interval (the core insight is a
variance-scaling argument — true volatility scales with `sqrt(Δt)` while the
spread contribution does not). When the underlying bars are already
1-minute-aggregated into a daily high/low, that scaling assumption is
violated, and the estimator cannot cleanly attribute the observed range to
spread vs. volatility. In our own testing this produced a **~135bp per-round-trip
estimate implying a ~40%/year cost drag** — an order of magnitude
larger than the measured real friction (13bp round-trip, §5.1–5.3) and larger
than the strategy's entire gross return, i.e. a cost model that would have
falsely killed a real edge.

**The lesson (stated once, generalizable).** A microstructure estimator that
depends on a specific sampling-frequency assumption is **not free to apply to
whatever OHLC granularity happens to be available** — the estimator's
internal identity can silently break when the input violates its
frequency assumptions, and the failure mode is not "noisy," it is
**systematically, wrongly large** (volatility gets misattributed to spread).
This is why §5.2–5.3 instead use (a) a square-root law anchored on directly
observed dollar volume (no range-decomposition assumption) and (b) the
Amihud ratio (a direct return-per-dollar-traded measure with no
high-low-range decomposition step at all). Both estimators are simpler,
make fewer distributional assumptions, and were checked against the ~13bp
round-trip empirical anchor rather than producing an unconstrained,
unvalidated number.

> **Invariant CS-REJECT — Corwin-Schultz is not used for impact/spread
> estimation in this system.** `src/convexity/cost/friction/validate/corwin_schultz_reject.py`
> keeps this as a standing regression test: re-running the Corwin-Schultz
> estimator on the 1-minute-aggregated daily OHLC panel must reproduce the
> documented ~135bp/~40%-drag garbage result (bounding it to the same order
> of magnitude), so that if anyone reintroduces Corwin-Schultz as a "spread
> estimator" for this book, the test fails loudly and points at this section.

### 5.5 Round-trip friction — the assembled rate

```
round_trip_friction_i = 2 · ( fee + half_spread_i / 2 + impact_frac_i )
```

exactly `src/convexity/cost/accounting.py::round_trip_friction`'s signature
(`taker`, `half_spread`, `impact`, each a per-leg fraction, doubled for
entry+exit legs), with `half_spread_i` and `impact_frac_i` now supplied by
the derived per-name model of §5.2–5.3 instead of the flat research
defaults `HALF_SPREAD_FRAC = 1e-4` / `IMPACT_FRAC = 0.5e-4`. At the
calibration anchor (median-liquidity name), the two are numerically
identical by construction:

```
round_trip_friction_median = 2 · (0.0005 + 0.0001/2 + 0.5e-4)
                            = 2 · (0.0005 + 0.00005 + 0.00005)
                            = 2 · 0.0006
                            = 0.0012  =  12 bp
```

Note the flat research baseline reports **13.0bp** round-trip
(`2*(5+1+0.5)bp`), i.e. `2·(0.0005 + 0.0001 + 0.00005)` — the half-spread
term there is **not** halved (the research default treats `HALF_SPREAD_FRAC`
as already the per-leg cost, not the full quoted spread). This spec's
`half_spread_i / 2` convention (§5.2, crossing cost = half the *quoted*
spread) and the research module's flat `HALF_SPREAD_FRAC` convention
(already a per-leg cost) are **two different parameterizations of the same
empirical ~1bp per-leg spread cost** — a builder wiring this spec into
`src/convexity/cost/accounting.py` must pick ONE convention consistently
(recommended: keep `src/convexity/cost/accounting.py`'s existing convention,
i.e. treat this spec's `half_spread_i` as already the per-leg cost, so no
extra `/2` is introduced — see Invariant CONV below) rather than composing
both, which would silently halve the half-spread term system-wide.

> **Invariant CONV — one half-spread convention, stated once, never
> composed.** Whichever of {"half_spread_i is the full quoted spread, halve
> it for one-sided crossing cost" , "half_spread_i is already the per-leg
> cost"} is chosen, it is chosen ONCE in `src/convexity/cost/friction/spread_impact.py`
> and the existing `round_trip_friction` call site is not further modified —
> composing this spec's derivation with an ALREADY-halved constant is the
> double-discount bug this invariant exists to catch. Default: match
> `src/convexity/cost/accounting.py`'s existing convention (no extra halving) so
> the derived model is a drop-in per-name replacement for the flat
> `HALF_SPREAD_FRAC` / `IMPACT_FRAC` constants at identical median-name
> magnitude.

### 5.6 Applying the per-name rate to ACTUAL turnover (P3, worked)

The friction is charged on the realized turnover vector, not on a fixed
per-name assumption. Two paths, both already implemented in
`src/convexity/cost/accounting.py::settle_week`:

**Scalar path** (flat rate across all names — the current production
default): `cost_t = turnover_t · round_trip_friction`, where `turnover_t =
0.5 · Σ_i |w_i,t − w_i,t-1_carried|`.

**Per-symbol path** (this spec's derived model, varying `round_trip_friction_i`
by name): `cost_t = 0.5 · Σ_i |w_i,t − w_i,t-1_carried| · round_trip_friction_i`,
which **reduces exactly to the scalar path** when `round_trip_friction_i` is
constant across `i` — the per-name model is a strict generalization, not a
parallel system.

**Worked example.** Suppose at a rebalance, name A (median liquidity,
`round_trip_friction_A = 13bp`) has `|Δw_A| = 0.04` (enters the long leg)
and name B (a tail name, `ADV_B = ADV_median/16` ⇒ `half_spread_B = 1bp ·
sqrt(16) = 4bp`, and by the matching Amihud scaling `impact_frac_B ≈ 4×
impact_frac_median = 2bp`, giving `round_trip_friction_B = 2·(5+4+2)bp =
22bp`) has `|Δw_B| = 0.04` (leaves the short leg). Both weight changes are
identical in magnitude, but:

```
cost_contribution_A = 0.5 · 0.04 · 0.0013 = 0.000026   (2.6 bp of book NAV)
cost_contribution_B = 0.5 · 0.04 · 0.0022 = 0.000044   (4.4 bp of book NAV)
```

The tail name costs **~1.7× more per unit of turnover** purely because its
`ADV` is 16× smaller — this is the mechanism by which expanding the universe
toward N=100 adds "real illiquid-tail cost" (`Convexity xsec momentum edge`:
~+2.9%/yr drag from the added tail) **without needing a separate universe-size
cost model**: the same per-name Amihud/sqrt-ADV machinery, applied to a wider
and thinner universe, mechanically produces a higher average
`round_trip_friction_i` and a correspondingly higher `cost_t`. This worked
example is the load-bearing derivation this spec commits to reproduce
bit-for-bit in a unit test (§9).

## 6. Funding — real 8h stamps, signed, minor term

### 6.1 Sign convention (derivation, not assertion)

A perpetual future's price is tethered to the underlying spot index by the
funding mechanism: periodically, longs pay shorts (or vice versa) an amount
proportional to the funding rate, which is itself a function of how far the
perp's price has drifted from spot (the premium). When the perp trades
**above** spot (positive premium, longs are "too eager"), funding is
**positive** and flows from longs to shorts — this pulls the perp price back
toward spot by making the long side progressively more expensive to hold
that state (a **cost-of-carry compensator**: the mechanism exists precisely
so that being long a rich perp costs something, discouraging the richness
from persisting). Symmetrically, a negative premium pays shorts to longs.

For a signed position weight `w_i` (positive = long, negative = short) and a
realized funding rate `f_i` at a given 8h stamp, the cash flow to the
position is:

```
funding_cashflow_i = -w_i · f_i
```

This is exactly `src/convexity/cost/accounting.py`'s `funding_cf = Σ (-w_target ·
f_acc)`. Checking the derivation against the mechanism above: `w_i > 0`
(long) and `f_i > 0` (perp rich, longs pay) gives `funding_cashflow_i < 0` —
the long position PAYS, consistent with the compensator argument. `w_i < 0`
(short) and `f_i > 0` gives `funding_cashflow_i > 0` — the short position is
CREDITED, consistent with being the side absorbing the rich-perp compensator
payment. This is P6's invariant, derived rather than merely stated.

### 6.2 Real wall-clock stamps, not per-bar or per-week lumping

```
funding_t = Σ_i  Σ_{τ_k ∈ (t, t+1week]}  -w_i,t · f_i(τ_k)
```

summed over the **actual** 8h funding timestamps that fall inside the
holding week (Binance USDT-M: 00:00, 08:00, 16:00 UTC — 3 stamps/day, ~21
stamps/week), each carrying its own **realized** rate `f_i(τ_k)` — never a
single lump-sum weekly average rate. `src/convexity/cost/accounting.py::_week_funding`
implements exactly this: it sums the **daily** funding array over
`(d0, d1]` (each daily entry itself already being the sum of that day's ~3
stamps as ingested from the panel), masked to days the name is `listed`, so
a name that delists mid-week stops accruing funding at its last live day
rather than accruing through a gap it was never actually exposed to.

> **Invariant F-stamp — funding is a sum over REAL stamps, never a single
> weekly-average rate.** A funding regime that is +0.01%/8h for 2 days then
> −0.02%/8h for 5 days must NOT be priced as a flat weekly average — the
> per-stamp sum captures the true path-dependent accrual (and, via the
> `listed` mask, correctly stops accruing at a mid-week delisting). Test:
> constructing a funding path with a mid-week sign flip and checking the
> per-stamp sum differs from (and is the economically correct alternative
> to) the flat-average approximation.

### 6.3 Momentum book funding is incidental, not a separate signal

Per `src/convexity/panel/sleeve.py`'s documented design, the **momentum**
signal's crypto sleeve deliberately runs with a **zero-funding assumption
for signal purposes** — funding carry is a SEPARATE signal
(funding-carry, tested and found to be an income-only satellite with
structural ~70–75% drawdown, DSR-failing; `Convexity xsec momentum edge`).
This spec's funding model (§6.1–6.2) still applies to the **accounting**
layer of the momentum book — the book DOES accrue real signed funding
cash flow on whatever weights the momentum signal happens to produce — but
COST does not tilt weights toward high-funding names on the momentum book's
behalf. That would silently import the failed carry signal's structural
drawdown into a book that was validated without it (Invariant SEP below).

> **Invariant SEP — funding accrues on the momentum book's incidental
> weights; it does not drive them.** `src/convexity/portfolio/portfolio.py`'s
> `quantile_weights`/`liquidity_weights` take no funding-rate input. A test
> asserts that permuting the funding-rate panel (holding price/rank fixed)
> changes `funding_t` in `settle_week`'s output but leaves `w_target` (and
> therefore `gross_price_t` and `turnover_t`) byte-identical.

### 6.4 Funding is empirically minor — the number, not just the claim

The empirical baseline states funding is **~3–5% of gross** return, versus
friction at **~10× larger**. Restated as a magnitude check on this spec's own
components: at 13bp round-trip friction paid ~52 times/year (~6.8%/yr cost
drag on 100% one-way turnover/week, less at realistic partial turnover), a
funding contribution at ~3–5% of *gross* (not of cost) is consistent with
funding sitting roughly one order of magnitude below the friction line when
gross returns and cost drag are of comparable scale — matching
`src/convexity/system/metrics.py::WeeklyMetrics`'s explicit separate reporting of
`cost_drag_per_year` and `funding_income_per_year` as independently
attributed lines (never summed into one unlabeled number, preserving the
prior COST.md's P5 discipline in spirit even though the FACT/ESTIMATE split
that motivated it no longer applies at weekly, already-realized cadence).

**Sanity cross-check against weekly win rate.** The empirical baseline's
weekly win rate is **~60%** (`WeeklyMetrics.win_rate_weeks`,
`src/convexity/system/metrics.py`). A friction line that is turnover-driven and
~10× the funding line predicts that win/loss weeks should **not** be
strongly separated by funding income — if losing weeks were disproportionately
the ones with adverse funding, that would indicate funding is a larger driver
of the *sign* of weekly P&L than its ~3–5%-of-gross magnitude suggests, and
would undercut P4's "turnover control, not funding timing, is what matters"
conclusion. This is not asserted here as a settled finding (the joint
win-rate/funding-sign breakdown has not itself been computed and is not in
the EVIDENCE digest by name) — it is recorded as a concrete, cheap
diagnostic COST's own reporting should carry: `src/convexity/cost/friction/validate/stats.py`
computes `win_rate_weeks` conditional on the sign of that week's
`funding_t` (two conditional win rates) alongside the unconditional
`win_rate_weeks ≈ 0.60`, so that a future divergence between the two
conditional rates is visible rather than silently absorbed into the single
blended number.

> **Invariant MINOR — funding is reported, never the reason a config passes.**
> SYSTEM's promotion criteria (§8) gate on **net-of-friction** Sharpe. COST
> does not currently ship a funding-ablation function anywhere in the
> codebase — `src/convexity/system/xsec_validate.py` contains only `purged_walkforward`
> and `deflated_sharpe` (thin wrappers around SYSTEM's walk-forward/DSR
> pillars); it has no funding on/off comparison today, and this spec must
> not claim otherwise. The ablation this invariant requires is **new,
> in-scope build work for this spec**: `src/convexity/cost/friction/friction_stress.py`
> (§3, §8) additionally exposes a `net_sharpe_ex_funding(net_weekly, funding)
> -> float` helper that recomputes Sharpe on `net_weekly - funding` (i.e.
> price-P&L-minus-cost only, funding zeroed out) using the identical
> annualization as `src/convexity/system/metrics.py::compute`. SYSTEM's promotion
> gate (`docs/specs/SYSTEM.md §6`) is the consumer that decides what to do
> with the two numbers; COST's obligation stops at emitting
> `net_sharpe_ex_funding` alongside the funding-inclusive net Sharpe so a
> config that clears promotion only via funding income (versus one that is
> friction-robust on price P&L alone) is visible in the report, matching the
> `Convexity xsec momentum edge` finding that carry alone is DSR-failing
> (PSR/DSR fails at 0.10–0.45 depending on overlay) and must never be the
> unstated reason a momentum config passes.

## 7. Universe size and the shortability caveat (a cost-adjacent constraint)

This is not a separate cost estimator, but a documented constraint on how
COST's own per-name friction model interacts with universe-size decisions,
because the empirical baseline is explicit that this is a **cost**, not a
signal, finding: expanding the universe from N=50 toward N=100 does **not**
robustly improve net Sharpe — net Sharpe peaks around **N=60–75** and then
*declines* toward N=100, driven by (a) the real illiquid-tail cost the §5.3
Amihud/sqrt-ADV model mechanically produces for thinner names (measured
**+2.9%/yr drag** from the added tail) and (b) short-availability: **~15 of
the +50 added names are effectively unshortable**, so part of the small
apparent gain from a larger universe leans on shorting names that cannot
actually be shorted at the assumed size — an implicit cost this spec's
friction model does not otherwise capture (a name that cannot be shorted at
all has effectively infinite impact cost on the short side, not merely a
high one). The honest N=50-vs-expanded-universe comparison shows **CI
overlap** (not a real edge) once this tail cost and shortability constraint
are both priced in.

**What `round_trip_friction_i` concretely returns for an unshortable name
(resolved, not deferred).** Neither `src/convexity/panel/panel.py` nor
`src/convexity/panel/sleeve.py` currently carries a short-availability/borrow
field (verified: no `short`/`borrow`/`shortable` attribute exists in either
module today) — the count of "~15 effectively unshortable names" is an
external empirical finding from the universe-size research, not yet a
column COST can read. Until that upstream metadata exists, COST's own
model must not silently produce a finite, tradeable-looking
`round_trip_friction_i` for a name that cannot actually be shorted: a
`sqrt(Amihud_i · Notional_leg)` impact estimate is a *liquidity-cost*
number, not a *feasibility* number, and returns a normal finite bps figure
regardless of borrow availability, because Amihud's ratio says nothing
about whether a short can be opened at all. This spec therefore commits to
one explicit, closed-form contract rather than leaving the behavior
implicit:

1. **Short-run (current state, no borrow metadata upstream):** COST's
   per-name model (§5.5) is defined only for names the portfolio layer has
   already selected into a leg; it has no way to distinguish an
   unshortable name from a shortable one and will return a normal finite
   `round_trip_friction_i`. This is a **known gap**, not a silent success —
   the "~15 unshortable names" cost is currently NOT priced by COST at all
   for any universe larger than the validated N~50 baseline (where the
   research finding is that unshortable names are concentrated in the
   +50 tail, not the core 50).
2. **Required fix, in scope for `src/convexity/cost/friction/`:** before COST's
   friction model runs on any universe config beyond N~50,
   `src/convexity/panel/sleeve.py` must expose a per-symbol-per-date
   `is_shortable: bool` (or equivalent borrow-availability flag) sourced
   from the exchange's actual borrow/short-interest metadata. Given that
   flag, `src/convexity/cost/friction/spread_impact.py`'s `round_trip_friction_i`
   returns `math.inf` (never `NaN`, never a large-but-finite placeholder
   bps figure) for any `i` flagged `is_shortable=False` at the rebalance
   date `t` for which the portfolio layer would otherwise place it in the
   short leg. `inf` is the correct value, not a modeling convenience: it
   forces `cost_t` (§3, `settle_week`) to reject that allocation
   arithmetically rather than silently pricing an infeasible trade at a
   finite cost, and it is upstream of, not a patch to, the portfolio
   layer's own leg-construction choice of whether to exclude such names
   before COST ever sees them (matching the "correct fix is to remove it
   from `U_t`, not to special-case it downstream" principle `PORTFOLIO.md`
   states for a structurally analogous case).
3. **A validation run may never report a universe-size "improvement" that
   was computed with unshortable names silently treated as normally
   shortable** — i.e. never with the short-run gap of item 1 quietly in
   effect while claiming an apples-to-apples N=50-vs-N=100 comparison.

> **Invariant N50 — universe-size decisions state the shortability
> constraint explicitly, and COST's own model returns `inf`, not a finite
> number, for a name it cannot short.** Any validation run comparing
> universe sizes (`src/convexity/system/xsec_validate.py`) must report, alongside net
> Sharpe, the count of short-leg names flagged unshortable at the
> evaluation date once the `is_shortable` metadata above exists, and must
> not report a universe-size "improvement" that is inside the
> block-bootstrap CI of the N~50 baseline as a real gain (matching the
> digest's explicit statement that the N=60–75 peak's gain over N=50 is
> inside the CI and therefore not a real improvement — the defensible
> simple choice is N~50). Until `is_shortable` metadata exists upstream,
> any report comparing N=50 to a larger universe MUST carry an explicit
> caveat that the comparison does not yet price the unshortable-name
> feasibility constraint and is therefore optimistic in the larger
> universe's favor.

## 8. The friction-stress promotion gate (feeds SYSTEM)

### 8.1 The scenario

For any config under promotion review, in addition to the baseline
net-of-cost Sharpe (using the friction rates of §5.5), COST supplies a
**stressed** friction scenario at **1.5× the baseline round-trip friction
rate**:

```
round_trip_friction_i^stress = 1.5 · round_trip_friction_i^baseline
```

applied through the identical `settle_week`/`round_trip_friction` machinery
— i.e. `src/convexity/cost/friction/friction_stress.py` re-runs the exact same weekly
settlement and Sharpe computation with only the friction rate scaled, never
re-fitting weights, ranks, or the lookback band under stress (a promotion
gate that lets the signal "adapt" to higher costs would no longer be testing
robustness of the ALREADY-CHOSEN config).

### 8.2 The promotion criterion

```
PASS  iff  Sharpe_net(friction = 1.5x baseline)  remains "economically meaningful"
```

Concretely, tied to the honest baseline (§8.4): the empirical Amihud-cost net
Sharpe is **~0.8–1.0** per lookback in the 12–16 band (`Convexity xsec
momentum edge`). Because a proportional friction increase on a book with
~10×-friction-dominant-over-funding cost structure (P4/§6.4) translates
roughly one-for-one into cost-drag, not into Sharpe collapse (cost is a
levelwise drag on mean return, not a volatility multiplier, so the Sharpe
degradation under a 1.5× friction stress is sub-proportional to the naive
"1.5× the cost line" intuition would suggest) — the promotion gate requires
the stressed Sharpe to remain **≥ 0.5** (retaining at least half of the
honest baseline's lower bound), and to remain **frac_pos ≥ 0.75** under the
same CPCV partition used for the baseline (§8.3) — a stricter bar than the
baseline's own `frac_pos = 0.85–1.00` (`Convexity xsec momentum edge`),
reflecting that a stress test should tighten, not loosen, the robustness
requirement. A config failing either sub-criterion is rejected at the
friction-stress stage regardless of its baseline-friction DSR/CPCV result.

> **Invariant STRESS — the friction-stress scenario changes ONLY the
> friction rate.** A test asserts that `friction_stress.py`'s stressed run
> and the baseline run share byte-identical `w_target`, `gross_price_t`, and
> `funding_t` series — only `cost_t` (and therefore `net_t`) may differ,
> scaled by exactly 1.5× on the friction component (funding and price P&L
> are untouched by a friction stress, per P4's separation of concerns).

### 8.3 Why frac_pos, not p05, is the binding statistic under stress

The CPCV split family behind these numbers is `src/convexity/system/cpcv.py::run_cpcv`'s
defaults: `N_groups=8, k_test=2, 28 paths, 1-week embargo` (the same
combinatorial-purged construction of López de Prado 2018 ch.12 used by
SYSTEM §4). At baseline friction, this run reports a **mean OOS path Sharpe
≈0.91 versus the naive single-path (full-sample) Sharpe of ≈0.98 — a haircut
of only ≈0.07** (`Convexity xsec momentum edge`; `SYSTEM.md §4.2` reports the
identical number from the identical CPCV run, since COST's baseline net
series *is* SYSTEM's validation input). This is a **small** haircut with
**no OOS collapse** — categorically unlike the pre-pivot directional-breakout
book's CPCV result, which flagged `luck_artifact=True` with OOS paths
straddling zero (`docs/EDGE.md`, `SYSTEM.md`'s provenance note). The
practical reading for COST: turnover/cost realism does not manufacture an
OOS collapse that isn't there — the honest Amihud-cost net series (§8.4)
degrades gracefully out of sample, which is precisely why a *further*
stress (§8.1's 1.5× friction multiplier) is a meaningful test rather than
one that is expected to immediately break a fragile result.

Per the same CPCV run, `frac_pos` (fraction of the 28 paths with positive
Sharpe) is the **robust** statistic (0.85–1.00 at baseline), while the
5th-percentile path Sharpe is **partition-fragile** (swinging +0.42 to −0.59
depending on the N-groups/k-test-groups choice) — `SYSTEM.md §4.3` derives
*why* `frac_pos` is the more stable statistic at this sample size (it is an
estimate of `P(SR_path > 0)` from a bounded indicator average, whereas `p05`
is an order statistic over only `P=28` paths and is therefore far more
sensitive to the tail of a small sample). A promotion gate — baseline or
stressed — that binds on p05 is binding on a statistic whose *value* depends
more on an arbitrary CPCV hyperparameter (N groups, k test groups) than on
the strategy's actual robustness. §8.2's stressed gate therefore binds on
`frac_pos ≥ 0.75`, mirroring SYSTEM's `Invariant CPCV-STAT` (`frac_pos`
gates, `p05` is reported but never a gating threshold) for statistics that
do not require re-litigating the CPCV partition scheme every time the gate
is evaluated.

### 8.4 The number to plan around vs. the number to cite as upper bound

This spec's numeric promotion thresholds (§8.2) are set against the
**honest, Amihud-impact-cost net Sharpe of ~0.8–1.0 per lookback**
(`Convexity xsec momentum edge`), NOT the light-cost/single-lookback headline
of **Sharpe ~1.6–1.9 / CAGR ~65%**. The headline number used a lighter
(flat, less-tail-punishing) cost proxy and a single fitted lookback rather
than the deployed band; it is cited here **once, explicitly labeled**, as
"optimistic upper bound, not the deployable estimate" — and is never to
reappear as a promotion-gate input, a reporting headline, or a sizing input
anywhere downstream of this spec.

> **Invariant HONEST-SR — the deployable Sharpe is the Amihud-cost,
> lookback-band number, cited by name every time it appears.** Any report,
> dashboard, or promotion decision quoting a Sharpe for this system states
> "~0.8–1.0, Amihud-cost lookback-band, `Convexity xsec momentum edge`" and,
> if the light-cost number is shown at all, labels it "optimistic upper
> bound — not deployable" in the same breath. A report showing only the
> light-cost number is a spec violation, not a rounding choice.

**Caveat this spec's own DSR consumer needs (not a COST result, but COST
feeds it and must not let it be misread).** COST's net-of-cost weekly series
is `src/convexity/system/xsec_validate.py::deflated_sharpe`'s direct input into
`src/convexity/system/validate/deflated.py::DeflatedSharpe`. `SYSTEM.md
§5.3–5.5` derives, against these same numbers, that DSR currently fails
**only on insufficient track record, not on a low point-estimate**:
`PSR≈0.994` (against `SR*=0`) is high — the *sign* of the edge is not in
serious doubt — while `n_obs≈258` weekly observations (~5 years) falls
short of the multi-testing-inflated `MinTRL` implied by the size of the
lookback/quantile/weighting search (`SYSTEM.md §5.5`). This is a
**track-length problem, not a deflation rejection**: a config whose DSR
gate reads "fail" for this reason has not had its edge refuted, it has
simply not yet accumulated enough weeks of live data to certify the edge at
the required confidence (`SYSTEM.md §5.4`'s "keep trading it, do not
re-derive it" reading). COST does not compute or gate on DSR/MinTRL itself
(§1, Invariant N) — this paragraph exists solely so a reader of COST's
honest-Sharpe number does not separately encounter a DSR "fail" from
`SYSTEM.md` and misattribute it to a defect in the cost model or the
Amihud-cost Sharpe estimate above; the two are different statistics
answering different questions (point Sharpe vs. confidence-adjusted
minimum track length).

## 9. Worked example — end to end (reproduce bit-for-bit)

One fully specified rebalance week, matching §5.6's per-name inputs, carried
through funding and the friction-stress scenario, so a builder can assert
against it.

**Inputs (all `F_t`-measurable at Friday close):**

| Input | Value |
|---|---|
| Book gross exposure | 100% (50% long + 50% short), dollar-neutral |
| Name A (median liquidity) | enters long leg this week, `Δw_A = +0.04` |
| Name B (tail liquidity, `ADV_B = ADV_median/16`) | leaves short leg this week, `Δw_B = +0.04` (was `-0.04`, now `0.00`) |
| `round_trip_friction_A` (§5.5) | `13.0 bp = 0.0013` |
| `round_trip_friction_B` (§5.6 worked calc) | `22.0 bp = 0.0022` |
| Name A funding over the week (7 real stamps used for illustration) | `f_A` sums to `+0.0009` over the week (net positive, A pays as it ends up net long) |
| Name A weight at settlement | `w_A = +0.04` |

**Per-name turnover cost (§5.6, per-symbol path):**

```
cost_contribution_A = 0.5 · |0.04| · 0.0013 = 0.000026   (0.26 bp of NAV)
cost_contribution_B = 0.5 · |0.04| · 0.0022 = 0.000044   (0.44 bp of NAV)
cost_t (these two names only) = 0.000070   (0.70 bp of NAV)
```

**Name A funding cash flow (§6.1–6.2):**

```
funding_cashflow_A = -w_A · f_A = -(0.04) · (0.0009) = -0.000036   (A pays 0.36bp of NAV)
```

confirming the sign: `w_A > 0` (long) and `f_A > 0` (net positive funding
over the week) yields a negative cash flow — A pays, as derived in §6.1.

**Friction-stress scenario (§8.1), same two names:**

```
round_trip_friction_A^stress = 1.5 · 0.0013 = 0.00195
round_trip_friction_B^stress = 1.5 · 0.0022 = 0.00330
cost_t^stress (these two names) = 0.5·0.04·0.00195 + 0.5·0.04·0.00330 = 0.000039 + 0.000066 = 0.000105  (1.05 bp of NAV)
```

i.e. exactly `1.5×` the baseline `cost_t` of `0.000070` (`0.000105 /
0.000070 = 1.5`), confirming Invariant STRESS's claim that only the friction
component scales — `funding_cashflow_A` is untouched by the stress scenario
(`-0.000036` in both the baseline and stressed run).

## 10. Edge cases and invariants (consolidated)

- **A name delists mid-week.** `src/convexity/cost/accounting.py::_asset_week_return`
  returns `NaN` for a name not `listed` at both week-boundary days; `w_end`
  drifts such a name's weight to `0` (position closed at its last live day).
  COST's funding model correspondingly stops accruing funding on that name
  at its last live day (`_week_funding`'s `listed`-masked sum), never across
  a delisting gap. (Tested by `Invariant F-stamp`'s companion delist case.)
- **A name has zero or near-zero trailing volume (thin `ADV_i`).** `half_spread_i
  = c/sqrt(ADV_i)` diverges as `ADV_i → 0`. The AMBER ingestion layer floors
  `ADV_i` at a minimum-eligible-liquidity threshold consistent with the
  universe construction's own liquidity filter (a name too thin to have a
  meaningful `ADV_i` should already be excluded from the ~50-name eligible
  universe upstream, per `src/convexity/panel/sleeve.py`) — COST does not invent
  a liquidity floor independent of the universe filter; it inherits it.
- **`Amihud_i` is undefined (fewer than a minimum window of trading days).**
  Falls back to the class-level flat research constant
  (`src/convexity/panel/sleeve.py::CRYPTO_FRICTION.impact`), never to zero and
  never to an extrapolated/fitted value from a thin sample (P5).
- **A name is selected into a short leg but cannot actually be borrowed
  (unshortable).** Resolved concretely in §7 (not deferred): COST's model
  returns `math.inf` for `round_trip_friction_i`, never `NaN` and never a
  finite placeholder bps figure, once the `is_shortable` metadata this spec
  requires (§7) exists upstream in `src/convexity/panel/sleeve.py`; until that
  metadata exists, this is a documented known gap (§7 item 1) — COST does
  not currently distinguish an unshortable name from a shortable one, and
  any report using COST's numbers on a universe wider than the validated
  N~50 baseline must carry that caveat explicitly.
- **The lookback-band ensemble (`L ∈ [12,16]`) changes turnover, not
  COST's model.** Each `L` in the band produces its own rank vector and
  therefore its own `w_target`/turnover path; COST prices whichever turnover
  each `L` produces with the identical §5 model — there is no per-`L` cost
  parameter (Invariant C-cfg).
- **Funding rate spikes to the venue cap.** `f_i(τ_k)` realized values are
  already clamped by the venue at the source (Binance enforces its own
  funding cap); COST's accrual (§6.2) sums whatever `f_i(τ_k)` the panel
  ingests — it does not need a second clamp on already-realized (FACT)
  funding, unlike a forward-looking forecast (which this spec, being
  realized-week accounting rather than intra-week forecasting, does not
  need to produce — see §11 non-goals).
- **Universe-size expansion changes the tail-cost distribution, not the
  model form.** Per §7, adding names does not require a new cost estimator
  — the same §5.2–5.3 machinery applied to thinner names mechanically
  produces higher per-name friction; the correct "fix" for a worse universe
  is a smaller/better-chosen universe (N~50, per the digest), not a patch to
  the cost model.
- **Friction-stress must not leak into weight formation.** Per Invariant
  STRESS, the 1.5× scaling happens strictly after `w_target` is fixed; a
  future temptation to "let the portfolio module see the stress scenario and
  trade less" would invalidate the promotion gate's meaning (it would be
  testing a different, adapted strategy, not the robustness of the one
  actually promoted).

## 11. Non-goals (explicitly out of scope for this spec)

- **No forward funding forecast.** Unlike the prior (directional,
  intraday-hold) COST.md, this book settles funding **realized**, one week
  at a time, at Friday close — there is no open position whose *future*
  funding needs forecasting mid-hold (Invariant M-hold from the prior spec
  is retired as inapplicable: the "unmeasurable hold length" problem does
  not exist when the hold is a fixed, known 7-day rebalance interval).
- **No per-trade net-EV gate.** There is no BREAKOUT-style entry decision
  this spec feeds a boolean gate to. Whether a config is "worth it" is
  decided by SYSTEM's promotion gate over the whole weekly return series
  (§8), not by COST vetoing individual name entries.
- **No execution/microstructure modeling.** This spec estimates *expected*
  impact for pricing the cost line (§5.3–5.4); it does not model order
  slicing, participation-rate scheduling, or live fill quality — there is no
  EXECUTION pillar in the xsec-momentum architecture (the book rebalances
  with a batch of taker orders at Friday close; the tactic-selection layer
  the old EXECUTION.md described does not exist in this pivot).
- **Non-crypto sleeves' cost model.** The equity/commodity research
  comparators (`src/convexity/panel/sleeve.py::EQUITY_FRICTION`,
  `COMMODITY_FRICTION`) remain flat conservative constants; extending the
  derived Amihud/sqrt-ADV model to those sleeves is future work, not part of
  this spec's scope (§Scope, top of document).
